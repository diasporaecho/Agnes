01

International schools

Match your actual subject, grade range, license and curriculum experience. Recruitment commonly starts well ahead of the school year; begin applications in the preceding autumn and keep monitoring spring vacancies. Two-year contracts are common, but every contract governs its own term.

02

Language & local schools

Check legal hiring eligibility, teaching registration, language fluency and whether a foreign qualification must be recognized. CELTA/TEFL/TESOL may help language roles; they do not automatically satisfy a regulated classroom teaching requirement. Public-school access is country-specific.

03

Online & tutoring

Get written employer/platform permission and check the host country’s work, tax and self-employment rules. A tourist stay or nonworking residence is not permission to earn online. University roles generally need subject-specific postgraduate credentials; no university eligibility is assumed.

Do not add housing to both cash income and expense savings. Deferred gratuity is not monthly spending money. A school’s “tax-free” label does not resolve U.S. taxes.

Build the application pack

  1. Two-page résumé with subject, grades, license validity and classroom results.
  2. Tailored cover letter connecting your experience to the curriculum and vacancy.
  3. Degree/transcript, license and employment evidence; send authenticated copies only when requested through a verified channel.
  4. Recent supervisors as references, with their permission, plus a small anonymized teaching portfolio.
  5. Prepare a demonstration lesson and questions about support, safeguarding, turnover, inclusion, workload and sponsorship.

Find and verify schools

Start with the country career links below, then compare vacancies on Search Associates, Schrole and TES. Recruiter participation is not a guarantee.

Confirm accreditation in the accreditor’s own directory, independently call the school, speak privately with current and former teachers, and check the legal employer. Legitimate recruiting memberships can have transparent fees; reject demands to pay a stranger for a guaranteed job or visa. Never wire money based only on a message or copied logo.

Every lifestyle budget is an authored scenario in USD, not a statistical average or a personalized quote. Modest means a smaller home or less central district; comfortable means a one-bedroom with a moderate social budget; higher comfort allows more space, dining and transport. City rents and housing examples in the country notes are separate evidence. Costs change by season, school location and age/health underwriting.

Upfront funds = estimated flights, documents, arrival accommodation and setup + two months of gross housing allowance as a deposit/advance placeholder + two months of modeled spending until pay arrives + your selected reserve months. The deposit assumption is not a local legal rule. An employer may reimburse later, so do not subtract a reimbursement before it reaches your account. Reserve uses the full budget without employer benefits because those benefits may stop with the job.

Protect the retirement you already earned

Before resigning, request a written pension estimate and confirm vesting, early-retirement reductions, service-credit rules, leave options, retiree health coverage, contributions and beneficiary information. A refund or rollover may permanently change pension rights. Do not assume overseas teaching adds U.S. state pension service.

Social Security coverage depends on the employer and any applicable agreement. Foreign credits may help eligibility under an agreement; they are not simply transferred into your U.S. record. Verify your own contribution history with the administrator and SSA. SSA guidance ↗

Taxes follow the facts

If a U.S. citizen or resident alien, worldwide income can remain reportable. Foreign earned income exclusion and foreign tax credits have conditions; neither is automatic and the same income cannot receive both benefits. Check destination tax residency, payroll, pension treatment, state domicile and self-employment obligations with a cross-border adviser. IRS guidance ↗

For a U.S. person, FBAR reporting generally applies when aggregate reportable foreign accounts exceed $10,000 at any point in the year. Other filings may also apply. FinCEN ↗

Banking, healthcare, medicines and ownership: checks before committing

Living well as a woman abroad

Visit a prospective neighborhood in daylight and after dark, test the actual return trip from school, and ask women on the staff about harassment, transport and building security. Check who handles workplace complaints and what independent labor or legal support is available. Choose a hospital and emergency contact before you need them. Build a small network through colleagues, language classes and community groups; country averages cannot tell you how one street or workplace will feel.

Banking & health

Ask the local bank for passport, residency, tax-number, address and employment requirements. Maintain a U.S. account only under its actual overseas-resident policy; keep two payment methods and compare full transfer fees and exchange spreads.

No medical needs or age are known. Insurance figures here are placeholders, not quotes. Obtain written coverage for disclosed conditions and medications, deductible, outpatient care, mental health, emergency transport and trips home. Verify medicine import rules with the destination health authority and consulate before carrying controlled prescriptions.

Buying & settling in

Choose a home based on the actual school commute. Use an independent local lawyer, verify land registry/title, liens, permits, condominium debts, seller authority and the contract language. Obtain a written closing-cost statement, mortgage preapproval and inheritance advice. Include maintenance, taxes, insurance, association fees, currency exposure and eventual resale costs.

Do not assume ownership grants residence or permission to teach. Price examples are asking prices, not valuations. Citizenship decisions can involve nationality-specific renunciation or retention rules; verify both countries’ treatment.

05 / TURN THE IDEA INTO A PLAN

One decision at a time.

Your first job is to test the fit. Your second is to verify the route. Moving comes after those answers are clear.

SCOUTING OPTION A / 10 DAYS

Bangkok, then Chiang Mai

Suggested only after school conversations and a current travel/health check. Days 1–3: base near a prospective Bangkok campus, ride the commute at school opening and dismissal, visit grocery stores and compare three apartments. Days 4–5: arranged school meetings, insurer/hospital checks and conversations with teachers. Days 6–8: fly to Chiang Mai, repeat neighborhood and school checks. Days 9–10: cost review and decision.

Assess air quality across seasons; a pleasant visit does not establish year-round suitability. Enter with the correct permission for the activities planned. No unpaid “trial teaching” without a legal check.

SCOUTING OPTION B / 10 DAYS

Mexico City, then Querétaro

Days 1–3: choose a school-first base and test peak-hour commutes, transit, groceries and evening return routes. Days 4–5: scheduled school interviews, three housing viewings, hospital/insurance research and resident conversations. Days 6–8: travel to Querétaro and repeat the exercise around actual school options. Days 9–10: compare net offers, budgets and paperwork with counsel before deciding.

Check current state-specific advisories and lawful entry requirements. These are two alternative scouting trips, not a recommendation to combine both countries in ten days.

6–12 MONTHS BEFORE

Confirm the foundation

Validate citizenship, license and subject fit. Request pension projections. Set the move budget. Shortlist schools and two cities; check actual work routes. Begin recruitment before the target school year.

3–6 MONTHS BEFORE

Secure the offer and route

Compare written contracts and net pay. Verify the school and sponsor. Order time-sensitive police checks, translations and apostilles in the sequence the authority requires. Apply through the responsible consulate.

FINAL 30 DAYS

Prepare a soft landing

Confirm lawful entry and start date, insurance, first payday, temporary arrival accommodation or completed home purchase, bank access, prescriptions, document copies and emergency contacts. Arrange mail, U.S. bills and tax records.

FIRST 30 DAYS

Get the essentials working

Complete local registration, residence-card and work-permit steps before teaching. Activate payroll, phone and healthcare. Verify the commute and lease/purchase handover. Save renewal dates.

FIRST 90 DAYS

Check the reality

Compare actual expenses with the budget. Review workload, insurance access and contract promises. Meet colleagues and community groups, practice the language and build routines beyond school.

EVERY YEAR

Keep your options open

Renew status and credentials on time. File applicable taxes and account reports. Review the pension, reserve, insurance and residence-day records. Recheck the long-term residence and citizenship rules.

Your move checklist

HAVE A WAY BACK

A move should leave you options.

Visa delayed or offer withdrawn: keep the reserve untouched, avoid nonrefundable long leases, obtain a revised start date and do not work before authorization.

Job ends: ask the authority about your exact grace period and change-of-employer process immediately; stop unapproved work and arrange independent insurance and housing.

Medical problem: know the covered hospital, insurer contact and evacuation terms. Keep a permitted medicine supply and medical summaries accessible.

Return home: reserve airfare plus an initial U.S. landing budget, keep documents and professional references, and plan lawful lease, tax and immigration closure.

Your next five actions

  1. Confirm citizenship, age, teaching license, subject and experience.
  2. Choose three schools with a lawful sponsorship route and request eligibility checks.
  3. Get a written pension estimate and an independent insurance quote.
  4. Compare actual net offers in the calculator, including U.S. obligations.
  5. Scout the leading city and authorize relocation only after the work/residence route is clear.
